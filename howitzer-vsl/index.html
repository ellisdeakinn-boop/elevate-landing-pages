On the examples

Every hook, number and client count on this page is illustrative, written to show the shape of a section. None of them are Markit results.

The premise

Most people put the pitch where nobody is left to hear it

Average VSL engagement sits between 20% and 30%. If the offer and the call to action live at the end, you are pitching to the 2 people in 10 who made it that far. Everyone else left without ever being told what you sell or how to buy it.

So the pitch moves to the front. The first 60 to 180 seconds carry who this is for, the big benefit, the outcome, why it works differently, the proof, and a button to click. Treat the first 30% of the video as a complete standalone sales pitch, because for most of your audience that is the whole video.

Who this converts

Write to the in-market buyer

This structure is built for someone who is already problem-aware, has tried other solutions, and is actively shopping right now. They want clarity, not free value. Give them a reason and they will move today.

  • They already know they have the problem. You do not need to sell the category.
  • They have hired someone before and been let down. Name that experience.
  • They can tell when they are being handled. Do not use 1930s pain-agitation on them.
  • They will pay well and be pleasant to work with, which is why the whole video is pitched at them and not at the general population.

The emotion you aim at decides the client you get

Aim at frustration, anger, stagnation, status, pride, purpose, willingness, desire. These are the emotions of someone who has agency and intends to use it.

Aim at fear, doubt, desperation or helplessness and you attract the people who see you as a lifeline. They pay the least and ask for the most. Someone great can still feel a low-frequency emotion on a bad day, so this is a spectrum, but if your whole campaign sits down there, that is the client base you build.

Before you write a word

Inputs

The script is a delivery system. The offer is the engine. If the offer is weak, a better script only gets you rejected faster.

  • The offer written out: what they get, what it costs you to deliver, the guarantee.
  • The avatar: industry, revenue, what they have already tried.
  • Sales call transcripts. This is the single highest-value input. It gives you their words and their objections, and you will lift lines from it directly.
  • Your proof: 3 named case studies with a number attached, plus a client count.
  • The one angle you are leading with. One video, one argument.

The language rule. Use their words, not the category's words. A roofer does not say "I struggle to get qualified leads predictably." He says "I can't get the phone ringing with homeowners who are ready to book." Read the transcripts and steal the phrasing.

The structure

11 parts

Every part has one job: hand the viewer the one thing that is still missing before they book. Each part has to stand on its own, because they will skim, skip, and scrub. Do not build it like a novel.

PART 010:00 to 3:00

The Hook

Give a ready buyer every piece of information they need to book, before they have a chance to leave.

Six jobs, all inside 3 minutes

  1. Name the ideal prospect so they know this is for them.
  2. State the big benefit your product or service delivers.
  3. Reinforce the outcome that follows from that benefit.
  4. Explain why this works differently to what they have already tried.
  5. Stack proof, relevance and uniqueness.
  6. Give the action takers a way to act right now.

Fill this in

If you're a [AVATAR], I will help you [SPECIFIC OUTCOME + NUMBER + TIMEFRAME]
through our [NAMED MECHANISM].

Just like we've done for [N] other [AVATAR]s like
[NAME], [NAME] and [NAME].

This way you can [WHAT THEY GET BACK: time, focus, headspace]
while [THE MACHINE DOES THE THING THEY HATE].

And if you've tried it all before, maybe you've even [THE FAILED
ATTEMPT THEY'VE ACTUALLY MADE]...

This is different, because [THE ONE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE:
risk reversal, pricing model, who carries the cost].

[SPELL OUT WHAT YOU ARE NOT PAID FOR]. [WHAT YOU ARE PAID FOR].

Worked example

"If you're a Chiropractor, I will help you get 30 to 60 new patient appointments every single month, through our attract, retain and refer strategy. Just like we've done for 134 other Chiropractors like John, Mary and Steven.

This way you can focus solely on patient care while your calendar gets filled with patients who are ready to pay for high-ticket care packages, and who have the money for it.

And if you've tried it all before, maybe you've even worked with a marketing guy who promised the world and delivered expensive, low-quality leads. This is different, because we pay for 100% of your ad spend. We only get paid when a patient pays for a high-ticket package. Not leads. Not calls. Not even appointments. Until we deliver cash for you, we lose money."

Example only. Never claim a number or a client you cannot show.

What the hook is not

You are not creating intrigue. You are not teasing. You are not bribing them to watch to the end. Assume they are on mute, between calls, half-skeptical and mostly ready to buy. Hand them everything and let them go.

PART 02Straight after the hook

Early CTA

Open a fast lane for the people who are already nodding, and give the rest permission to keep watching without feeling sold at.

Some viewers are bought in by the end of the hook and only need permission to act. Without this checkpoint you lose the ones who needed a small push. It also tells the undecided that they have already heard the important part, which keeps them relaxed instead of guarded.

Worked example

"Now if that sounds like exactly what you've been looking for, and you already know this is something your business needs, there's a button below this video where you can book a call. We'll walk through your business, see if you're a fit, and I'll show you exactly how we'd roll this out for you, start to finish.

If you're still on the fence, or you just want to make sure this isn't another one of those marketing things, totally fine. Stick around and I'll show you how it works and why it performs."

PART 03

The Introduction

Establish, at a high level, why a stranger should listen to you. It also gives them a breathing point after the hook dumped everything on them at once.

Fill this in

Let me take a step back and tell you who I am and why you should
listen to me at all.

My name is [NAME], I'm [ROLE] at [COMPANY].

I've personally [VOLUME METRIC: scripts written, accounts managed,
builds shipped], we've generated [HARD NUMBER] for our clients,
and we've worked with [RECOGNISABLE NAMES].

I'm not telling you this to brag. I just want to establish why I'm
qualified to talk about this in the first place.

Keep it to your name, your experience, one or two lines of implied authority, and why you are qualified on this specific topic. Nothing about where you grew up unless it changes what you can do for them.

PART 04

The Core Problem

Describe their situation so accurately that they feel understood. Do this well and the rest of the video barely matters.

You have had the experience of sitting with friends while someone describes a situation you have lived, and you start finishing their sentences because finally somebody gets it. That is the effect you are after. If you can articulate their problem the way they would describe it themselves, they will believe you can solve it.

Remind them, quietly, how the problem shows up in their business, in their day, and in their own head. Pain drives decisions. But keep it objective and keep it high frequency.

Pitch the pain at the right level

He's questioning why he ever started this business, and his wife is doubting him.The business has plateaued, he can see exactly where it's stuck, and it's irritating him every single day.

Lift lines from your call transcripts

PART 05

The False Solution

Break the mental model they are operating on, and replace it with one that makes your mechanism the obvious answer.

The two sentences

[Problem] isn't because of [common cause], it's actually
because of [uncommon cause].

Most people think the solution to [problem] is [common solution],
but it's actually [uncommon solution].

This runs on cognitive dissonance. The brain cannot hold two contradictory ideas comfortably, so it has to resolve the tension. You create the tension by showing them that what they believe is flawed, then immediately hand them a belief that is clearer and easier to act on.

You have to earn this one

The move only works if you have already proven three things: that you know what you are talking about, that you understand their specific situation, and that you are the right person to listen to. That is why Part 04 comes first.

Without that, the brain resolves the tension the cheap way, by deciding you are full of it. Your grandmother telling you Coke Zero is worse than Coke changes nothing. The same claim from someone who has spent millions studying longevity, with a mechanism attached, makes you look at the can in your hand.

Worked example

"Most IT companies think the reason they can't scale is that they need more leads. So they hire cold callers, buy lead lists, run ads, and end up stuck on one-off jobs at $10k to $20k MRR while working 14 hour days.

In reality you're stuck because you're charging $100 per seat, you have no real sales process, and you have no offer that justifies charging $300. If you did, you'd make far more without hiring anyone or bolting 100 more services onto the package."

Name what they have tried, give a logical reason it did not work, and lay out why your approach is built differently. Skip the "it's not your fault" routine. They can smell it.

PART 06

The Real Solution and Future Pacing

Explain why this category of solution works. Not your offer yet, the reason behind it.

You are building the perfect opportunity in their head. Describe the qualities the right solution has to have, then paint what their week looks like once it is running.

Fill this in

What you need is [THE CATEGORY OF SOLUTION], built for
[HOW THEIR BUYERS ACTUALLY BEHAVE].

Something you don't have to spend months building.
Something you can run yourself, so your leadflow isn't hostage to
someone else.
Something with a guaranteed return, so you're not lighting money
on fire hoping it works.
Something live in days, not months.

And when you have that:
  - [OUTCOME IN THEIR PIPELINE]
  - [WHAT THEY STOP RELYING ON]
  - [WHAT THEIR MORNINGS LOOK LIKE]
  - [WHAT THEY STOP REBUILDING]
  - [THE TIME THEY GET BACK]
PART 07

Reinstate the Big Promise

Restate the promise from the hook, now that they understand what it actually means.

They have seen the problem, the false solution, and the real one. Say the promise again, plainly, and tie it to the mechanism you just explained. Keep it benefit-led. The deliverables come next.

Worked example

"And yes, this is exactly what we install inside 7 Figure MSP. It's a plug and play sales and marketing system built for MSPs, designed to double your MRR in 60 to 120 days. A system that doesn't run on referrals or word of mouth. A sales process that lets you charge $300 a seat instead of $100. And a repeatable offer that turns cold traffic into monthly revenue without chasing or discounting."

PART 08

Case Studies and Proof

Answer the only question left: has this worked for someone like me?

Three case studies, 2 sentences each, 3 elements

  1. Who you helped and what industry they are in.
  2. What you helped them do, with a number.
  3. How you did it, in one clause.

Fill this in

We helped [NAME] [SPECIFIC RESULT WITH A NUMBER] after
[THE ONE THING YOU CHANGED].

[Repeat twice more.]

And [N] other clients we've helped [THE PATTERN RESULT].

Put their faces and result screenshots on screen while you say it. Then do the thing nobody else does:

"Their handles are on screen and in the case studies section below this video. Go and message them, ask what it was like working with us. We haven't paid them. They don't even know I'm handing out their socials, I'm just that confident in the work we did."

PART 09

List What They Get

Close the gap between "I believe the outcome" and "I can see how it physically arrives."

Promising an outcome is not enough. They cannot see it or touch it. A pizza oven that says "everything you need to make pizza" on the box tells you nothing. A list saying peel, coal, non-stick grate, gloves does. List the contents.

Rules for this section

  • List features plainly. This is the logical justification, not a performance.
  • Add a "so that you can" line only where the benefit is not obvious. "30 days of coaching so that when you hit a roadblock you message me and it's solved that day" earns it. "Your entire funnel built for you" does not need one.
  • Never put a fake price on a line item. No "valued at $497". It reads as a trick and it costs you the trust you spent 8 minutes building.
  • Make it feel like a lot by being complete, not by inflating it.
PART 10

Objections, Quickfire

Say the one thing that is still holding them back, out loud, before they have to think it.

Pull the list straight from your sales call transcripts. Name each objection and answer it honestly. Move fast. This is a rapid burst, not a chapter.

Fill this in

Look, if you've watched this far and you still haven't booked,
it's probably because you've got a question. Here are the ones
we get most.

[OBJECTION] - [HONEST ANSWER]
[OBJECTION] - [HONEST ANSWER]
[OBJECTION] - [HONEST ANSWER]
PART 1130 to 60 seconds

Final CTA

Close the loop and make booking the obvious next step for anyone who has watched this far.

Seven beats

  1. Reopen the pain loop in one sentence. "If you're still stuck with [status quo]..."
  2. Restate the transformation. "...and you want to [main outcome]..."
  3. Tie it to the offer. "That's exactly what we do with [product]."
  4. Say what happens on the call. "We'll look at X, Y and Z, and I'll show you how we'd roll it out."
  5. Lower the friction. "If it makes sense we move forward. If not, no hard feelings, and you still leave with [deliverable]."
  6. Stack the deck. "If I'm wrong, you lose 30 minutes. If I'm right, you join the [N] others who [outcome]."
  7. Tell them what to do. "Hit the button, pick a time, let's talk."

Worked example

"So if you're still stuck charging $150 a seat, living off referrals, chasing one-off break-fix jobs, and you want a real sales system that closes $300 a seat retainers, that's exactly what we build with you inside 7 Figure MSP.

We work with you one to one to reposition the offer, build the sales assets, turn one-off jobs into recurring revenue, and double your MRR in 60 to 120 days. Just like we've done for over 2,000 MSPs.

Hit the button below to book a free audit call. We'll look at your pricing, your offer and your sales process, and I'll show you what's broken and how we'd fix it. If it makes sense, I'll walk you through the program and the pricing. If not, no pressure, you'll leave with advice on what we'd do in your shoes.

If I'm wrong, you lose 30 minutes of your day. If I'm right, you join 2,000 other MSPs scaling predictably. So hit the button, pick a time, and let's talk."

Variant

Running this for a low-ticket offer

The 11 parts above assume the conversion event is a booked call. When it's a checkout button on the same page, the frontloading holds and 6 of the parts change. Here is what moves.

The price goes in the hook

On a book-a-call funnel you hold the number because the call is where qualifying happens. On a checkout funnel the number is a reason to act. A low price removes the biggest source of hesitation a cold viewer has, which is not knowing what this is going to cost them. Say it in the first 60 seconds, say it again at the end.

The guarantee does the job the sales call used to do. It carries the risk so the viewer does not have to.

What changes, part by part

PartChange
01 The HookAdd the price and the guarantee. Everything else in the six-job list stays exactly as written.
02 Early CTAPoints at a buy button. The path from watching to owning is one click, so a bigger share of your buyers close right here. Name the price again.
03 The IntroductionCut it to 3 or 4 lines. A $49 decision does not need your full résumé, it needs to know you are a real operator.
04 The Core ProblemUnchanged. Still the section that earns you the right to everything after it.
05 The False SolutionUnchanged. This is the engine of the whole video at any price.
06 The Real SolutionCompress. Fewer future-pacing lines, because you have less belief to build for a small spend.
07 The Big PromiseUnchanged.
08 ProofVolume beats depth. "1,400 people have run this" does more work than 3 deep case studies when the buyer is risking the price of lunch.
09 What They GetThis becomes the biggest section in the video. On a checkout there is no call to answer "what do I actually receive", so the contents list has to. Name every module, format and access detail.
10 ObjectionsDifferent objections. They ask "is this 40 videos of theory", "how long is this going to take me", and "do I need an ad budget to use it".
11 Final CTAPrice, what happens after they pay, guarantee terms, button. No calendar.

A cheap product does not mean a short video

The instinct is to keep it short because the price is small. Length follows the buyer. The price tag has nothing to do with it. Low-ticket cold traffic usually skews to the analytical end, and that audience wants more detail and more reassurance before it moves, not less. Check the calibration table below and pick off the audience, then cut anything that is not doing a job.

Three things that quietly kill low-ticket VSLs

  • Holding the price back. A viewer who cannot find the number assumes it is high and leaves. You lose the exact buyer the low price was built to catch.
  • Inflated value stacks. "$2,940 of value, yours for $49" reads as a trick at any price, and it costs you the trust you spent 8 minutes earning. List the contents and let them add it up.
  • Selling the whole ladder in one video. Sell the one thing on the page. The order bump, the upsell and the retainer all live downstream. A VSL that tries to sell the $49 product and the $4,000 service closes neither.
Calibration

How long, and how many words

Length follows the buyer, not the framework. Wealthier, faster-moving audiences want it short. Analytical audiences want the detail and will sit through it.

AudienceRuntimeScript length
Affluent, decisive, already aware2 to 10 min750 to 2,200 words
Analytical, skeptical, less aware15 to 30 min2,250 to 6,600 words
Cold, problem-unaware30 to 45 min6,600 words and up

Talking-head delivery runs around 220 words a minute. Write at a 4th to 5th grade reading level. One idea per sentence. If a sentence has two ideas in it, split it.

On price

Hold the price for a book-a-call funnel, because the call is the conversion event and the qualifying happens there. State it for anything with a checkout on the page. The funnel type decides it, not squeamishness about the number. The checkout variant is spelled out above.

Before you record

Checklist

  • A viewer who watches only the first 3 minutes knows who it's for, what they get, why it's different, and how to book.
  • There is a call to action inside the first 3 minutes, and again at the end.
  • Every section makes sense on its own and gives one more reason to book.
  • The problem section uses language lifted from real sales calls, not category language.
  • The false solution names something they have genuinely tried.
  • 3 case studies, each with a name, a number and a method.
  • Deliverables listed as a concrete contents list, with no invented dollar values.
  • Objections answered honestly, in a burst, near the end.
  • Every number, client name and result is real and you can show it on screen.
  • On a checkout funnel, the price and the guarantee are both inside the first 60 seconds.
  • Read it out loud start to finish. Anything you would not say out loud gets cut.

The Howitzer structure is built for one job: cold traffic into a booked call for a high-ticket offer. The theory carries across to other funnels, the running order does not. If the conversion event changes, rebuild the sequence around it.
